- Design rationale for the non-obvious choices, one line per decision of the plan (decisions 2 to 11), and a line for the three others:

  - Decision 2, cycles by an iterative Tarjan search: G13 names strongly connected sets, and Tarjan's algorithm finds them in one depth-first pass, linear in topologies and arcs. F1 measured why: a recursive variant finds a ring of 3000 topologies and overflows the stack at 10000 under `dart test`, a depth a long catalog chain reaches, while the iterative search finds 300000 in 676 ms; no conformance case comes near it (the largest G13 case builds seven topologies).   - Decision 3, arcs: an arc runs from topology i to topology j, j not i, when j reads a topic i writes, of any variant, from one index of readers per topic in graph order; claims take no part, and a self-read is no arc, so a self-read is not a cycle of one.   - Decision 5, cycles sorted by first id after the search: Tarjan completes components in reverse topological order, not in id order, and Task 1's first test builds a graph where the search from the first topology completes the later-named cycle first.   - Decision 6, self-references by two passes: a stage input names an earlier edge (G6), so a definition's edges are in dependency order. ## Learnings

- topology-ir@v1 orders scope violations "by topic, then owner, then reader" without saying how two owners of one source in two scopes order (one service claiming unbound and bound to a module, S143's contract gap, hsdd/contract/topology-ir.md line 436), no fixture builds that tie (the suite's G16 case has equal page claims and owners of different sources only), and this phase breaks the tie by scope after the source's rendering as decision 8 records, so an owner sort by source alone passes the gate's replay (31 of 31, re-run here) and fails only `G16 owners of one source come by scope however many owners a topic has`. Disposition: dropped (routed to the end-of-node governance sweep for topology-ir@v1)
