# ADR-003: Time source and virtual scheduler on Dart

## Context

Spec §3.6 has the bus accept a `SchedulerProvider` at construction, defaulting to the platform's real-time scheduler and replaced in `TestBus` by a `VirtualScheduler` with `advanceTimeBy`, `advanceTimeTo` and `triggerActions`. It requires every time-dependent operator inside topologies (`debounce`, `throttle`, `delay`, `retryWhen`, `timeout`, `sample`) to use the injected scheduler, so that tests are fully deterministic. Rationale §3.2 makes that determinism a stated property: time-dependent operators are the one controlled source of non-determinism in a topology, made deterministic by injecting a virtual scheduler. Spec Appendix A.7 defines `TestBus` as a bus configured with a `VirtualSchedulerProvider` that records every envelope, and Spec §3.1 gives each test case its own. Guide §10.1 leaves the clock-control API and its interaction with Flutter's `WidgetTester` open. Root OQ7 asks how the injection maps onto Dart; root OQ2 inherits Guide §10.1.

Dart gives the injection nothing to attach to. RxDart 0.28.0 defines no scheduler type, although Guide §6.5 names an `EventLoopScheduler` for Dart, and its time operators create `dart:async` timers directly (`docs/auto-run/experiments/time-source/output-rxdart-schedulers.txt`). Dart offers the zone instead: a timer, a periodic timer or a microtask is created through the zone current at that moment, and a stream subscription runs its callbacks in the zone that was current when `listen` was called. The time-of-day counterpart is `package:clock`, whose `clock` is a zone value that `fake_async` and `withClock` override; `DateTime.now()` is never virtualized. ADR-002 lists `clock` among the runtime's dependencies and leaves `fake_async` out, and requires the runtime's `testing.dart` and the Flutter adapter's widget-test helpers to import neither `package:test` nor `package:flutter_test`. Root D9 records that a `sync: true` behavior or replay subject delivers its replay to a new subscriber in a microtask, and Spec §3.4 defers reentrant publishes with `scheduleMicrotask`. Spec §7.3 sets a 100 ms default grace timer, Spec §3.13 a 5 s dev-mode window, and Spec §11.2 has a test shut its bus down in `tearDown`.

The root decomposition proposed this ADR on the strength of `docs/auto-run/experiments/time-source/` and `time-source-flutter/`: `fake_async` controls RxDart timers only for subscriptions made inside its zone, nested fake zones keep separate clocks, a zone-based scheduler the bus owns controls `debounceTime`, `throttleTime` and `delay` and captures the replay microtask and the deferral, and under `testWidgets` a pass-through zone lets `tester.pump` drive the bus while a virtual zone stays independent of it. Two further experiments, each with its source and captured output, settle what those had not checked.

`docs/auto-run/experiments/virtual-scheduler/` (VM; the prototype is `lib/scheduler.dart`, the run `output.txt`):

- Inside the provider zone and fed from outside it, `debounceTime`, `throttleTime`, `delay`, `sampleTime` and `bufferTime` (both on `Stream.periodic`), `Stream.timeout`, `interval`, `Rx.retryWhen` with a timed retry, and a `Stream.periodic` created in a `switchMap` mapper are driven by virtual time alone and emit nothing in real time. Cancelling each subscription leaves no timer pending. A shell stream fed by a timer the service created outside the zone runs on real time (Q1).
- RxDart 0.28's `switchMap`, when it switches while the previous inner stream is live, chains on the previous inner subscription's `cancel()` future. When nothing is pending, `cancel()` returns the SDK's shared completed future, which `dart:_internal` creates with `Zone.root.run`, so the continuation that listens to the next inner stream is scheduled on the root microtask queue. The virtual scheduler never sees it: after one real turn the next inner is listened at whatever virtual time has been reached by then (Q1). The same hop leaves `switchMap` without its next inner for the rest of a `fakeAsync` block (Q9) and of a `testWidgets` body (`time-source-widget` W4). A `switchMap` that requests the previous cancel without waiting for it and listens to the next inner at once keeps exact virtual timing in all three (Q1, Q9, W4b). No other operator among the Spec §6.3 combinators chains on a cancel future in RxDart 0.28.
- A stream whose `listen` re-enters the provider zone gives a consumer listening from its own zone the replayed first value under `triggerActions`, without a real turn, and runs the consumer's callbacks in the provider zone; the raw subject stream needs a real turn. Operators a consumer chains onto such a stream are virtual when RxDart creates their timers during delivery (`debounceTime`, `sampleTime`) and real when it creates them at `listen` (`Stream.timeout`) (Q2).
- `zone.scheduleMicrotask` and `zone.run(() => Timer(...))` put work into the virtual queue from a call made in another zone; the global `scheduleMicrotask` does not (Q3).
- The prototype drains queued microtasks first, in order, then each due timer in due-time and creation order, running the microtasks it scheduled before the next timer, as the real event loop does. A timer created during an advance and due within it fires within it; a 300 ms periodic timer fires three times over one second; the argument checks, the reentrancy check and the action bound behave as intended. Errors from scheduled work reach the error zone the scheduler was built in, not the zone that called the operation, and the drain continues (Q4). `fake_async` 1.3.3's `elapse` never returns while a zero-period periodic timer is pending (`output-fake-async.txt`).
- Inside a zone forked under `withClock(virtualClock, ...)`, `clock.now()` reads virtual time (Q5).
- A provider that captures `Zone.current` and `clock` at construction follows `fakeAsync` when built inside it and real time when built outside it, and keeps the `withClock` clock it was built under, wherever it is read from (Q6).
- A stub the test completes from its own zone reaches the virtual zone only across a real turn; an asynchronous settle that alternates `triggerActions` with real turns delivers it (Q7).
- Delivering through a pass-through fork of the root zone costs about 1.3 times as much per event as delivering in the captured root zone, about 4 ns more per event (Q8).

`docs/auto-run/experiments/time-source-widget/` (Flutter; `run.sh`, `output.txt`):

- A provider that captures `Zone.current` inside a `testWidgets` body is driven by `tester.pump` (W1). There, `clock.now()` starts at the wall-clock time of the test start and advances exactly with `tester.pump`, while `DateTime.now()` does not move (W2).
- A provider captured in `setUp` sits outside the body's fake zone, and `tester.pump` does not drive it (W3).
- When the body ends, the binding unmounts the tree, descendants first, and then fails the test if a fake timer is still pending ("A Timer is still pending even after the widget tree was disposed"), before tear-downs run, so an `addTearDown` that would cancel the timer comes too late (`pending_timer_test.dart`). A grace timer that a descendant starts in its `dispose` and an ancestor cancels in its own `dispose` leaves nothing pending (W5), and neither does an RxDart debounce subscription cancelled in `dispose` (W6).

## Decision

Resolves OQ7 and the clock-control part of OQ2.

1. **The provider is a zone and a clock.** On Dart, the `SchedulerProvider` of Spec §3.6 is a pair: a `Zone`, through which every timer, periodic timer and microtask the bus causes is created, and a `Clock` from `package:clock`, from which every time value the bus produces is read. A bus holds one provider, fixed at construction. Exactly two providers exist; applications cannot define another.
   - The **production provider**, `BusConfig`'s default, is the zone current when the bus is constructed, used as it is without a fork, and `package:clock`'s `clock` as read at that moment. Both are captured when the bus is constructed, not when the config value is built. It intercepts nothing. A bus constructed inside `fakeAsync`, inside `withClock` or inside a `testWidgets` body therefore runs on that zone's timers and that clock, wherever its operations are later called from.
   - The **virtual provider**, `VirtualSchedulerProvider`, wraps a `VirtualScheduler` (item 5).

2. **The bus works inside the provider zone.**
   - Every subscription the bus makes, for topology activation and for its internal wiring, is made inside the provider zone.
   - `bus.observe(topic)` returns a stream whose `listen` subscribes inside the provider zone, so a subscriber's callbacks run there whatever zone it listens from. This is the Dart form of what Spec §3.3 has `bus.observe` do on Combine: subscribe on the topic's delivery dispatcher.
   - Observers (Spec §3.7) are invoked inside the provider zone.
   - The bus creates its own timers and schedules its own microtasks in the provider zone explicitly, whatever zone the call arrives in: the reentrancy deferral (Spec §3.4); the startup barrier's dev-mode window (Spec §3.13), created by the first queued publish and cancelled by `start()`; the module grace timer (Spec §7.3). A grace period of zero tears the module down synchronously on the last release, with no timer.
   - `shutdown()` cancels every timer the bus created and, by cancelling every pipeline subscription, every timer its pipelines created. No such timer stays pending after it.

3. **Nothing leaves the provider zone.** Work that a bus operation or a topology pipeline starts completes inside the provider zone: no nidana code awaits or chains on a future created outside it, and none chains on the future `StreamSubscription.cancel()` returns, which is the SDK's root-zone future when nothing is pending. The live `switchMap` of the topology DSL cancels the previous inner subscription without waiting for its cancel future and listens to the next inner stream at once. RxDart 0.28's `switchMap` waits for that future, so the runtime does not use it unchanged.

4. **Time-dependent combinators are RxDart's.** The topology DSL's time-dependent combinators, whichever of debounce, throttle, delay, sample, buffer, timeout and timed retry topology-dsl@v1 names, are RxDart 0.28's operators or `Stream.timeout` from `dart:async`, applied inside the pipeline. They take no scheduler argument. Because the pipeline subscribes inside the provider zone, every timer they create, periodic ones included, is a provider-zone timer, and cancelling the pipeline cancels it.

5. **The virtual scheduler.** `VirtualScheduler` is nidana's own, in the runtime's `testing.dart`; no nidana package depends on `fake_async`. It owns a zone, forked from the zone current when the scheduler is constructed, that intercepts `createTimer`, `createPeriodicTimer` and `scheduleMicrotask` and never forwards them, and a virtual clock, which is also `package:clock`'s `clock` inside that zone. Virtual time starts at a fixed instant, whose default bus-testing@v1 names and which the constructor can override; it never reads the wall clock and never moves backward. The three operations of Spec §3.6 are synchronous:
   - `triggerActions()` runs queued microtasks in order, and timers due at the current virtual time in due-time and creation order, running every queued microtask before each timer, until no microtask is queued and no timer is due. Virtual time does not move.
   - `advanceTimeBy(duration)` runs `triggerActions()`, then repeatedly moves virtual time to the earliest timer due at or before the target and runs `triggerActions()` there, then sets virtual time to the target and runs `triggerActions()` once more. A periodic timer fires once for each period crossed, and a timer created during the advance and due within it fires within it. A negative duration throws `ArgumentError`.
   - `advanceTimeTo(timestamp)` is `advanceTimeBy` up to that instant; an instant before the current virtual time throws `ArgumentError`.
   - Calling an operation from inside work the scheduler is running throws `StateError`. An operation that runs more actions than a bound, whose default bus-testing@v1 names, throws `StateError`; that is how a microtask loop or a zero-period periodic timer shows up.
   - Scheduled work runs guarded: an exception goes to the error zone the scheduler was constructed in, and the operation carries on.
   - When an operation returns, the scheduler is quiescent: no microtask is queued and no timer is due at the current virtual time. Timers due later stay pending; the scheduler reports how many are pending and when the next one is due, so a test can assert that a timer was cancelled.

6. **TestBus.** `TestBus(config)` is a bus whose provider is virtual: `config`'s provider when it is a `VirtualSchedulerProvider`, a fresh one when `config` leaves the default. A production provider is rejected with `ArgumentError`. A TestBus exposes its scheduler.
   - Everything that bus operations, topology pipelines and `bus.observe` subscriptions schedule is delivered by the scheduler operations alone. A test that drives a TestBus through bus operations and scheduler operations therefore runs synchronously, without `await`, including the first delivery of state and replay topics, which comes in a provider-zone microtask (bus-api@v1 G17, which resolved OQ-R1).
   - Work that starts outside the provider zone reaches it only across a real event-loop turn: a future the test completes, a timer the test or a shell service creates outside a bus callback, an operator that waits on a future created outside the zone, such as RxDart's `switchMap` chained onto an observed stream. For such tests bus-testing@v1 provides an asynchronous settle operation that alternates `triggerActions()` with real event-loop turns until the scheduler stays quiescent across a turn, up to a bound. The `settle()` of an ADR-001 harness over a TestBus uses it. Settling needs a real event loop, so it is never awaited inside `fakeAsync` or a `testWidgets` body.
   - The envelope recorder attaches to a bus with either provider, so a widget test records envelopes on a production-provider bus.

7. **Flutter widget tests run the production provider.** `tester.pump(duration)` then drives every bus timer and the bus's clock.
   - The bus is constructed inside the test body; one constructed in `setUp` or at file level runs on real time.
   - No bus timer may be pending when the body ends, because flutter_test fails the test once it has unmounted the tree, before tear-downs run. The adapter's widget-test helpers construct the bus in the body and shut it down from the `dispose` of a widget above every scope binding, which runs after the bindings' own `dispose` has released their scope references. A test that constructs its own bus shuts it down before the body ends.
   - A widget test never puts a `VirtualScheduler` behind the bus: `tester.pump` would not move its clock.

8. **Time values come from the provider clock.** Envelope timestamps (Spec §2.4), the dev-mode window, the route transition timestamps navigation executors write, and the durations the telemetry observer measures, through the clock's `stopwatch()`, all come from the provider clock. bus-api@v1 exposes the provider clock, read-only, to shell code that stamps values, so an executor's timestamps share the bus's clock. No nidana package calls `DateTime.now()`, constructs a `Stopwatch` directly, or creates a timer or microtask outside a provider zone. `nidana_dart_contracts` reads no clock and schedules nothing: a function that needs a timestamp or a fresh id takes it as an argument.

9. **One dispatcher per bus.** On Dart, Spec §3.6's per-topic delivery dispatcher is the provider zone on the bus's isolate, the same for every topic. No per-topic dispatcher choice exists, and `bus.observe` performs no dispatcher hop (Spec §11.1).

## Consequences

- On a TestBus, topologies and every bus operation are deterministic (Rationale §3.2) and need neither real time nor `await`. A test that stubs a shell service with futures it completes itself awaits the settle operation.
- TestBus tests stub every effect. Real I/O started inside the provider zone delivers only when a scheduler operation or the settle operation runs after it arrives, and a shell service's own timers created outside bus callbacks run on real time.
- A `bus.observe` subscriber's callbacks run in the provider zone, not in its own: zone values it set are not visible there, and errors in those callbacks reach the error zone the bus was constructed in. An app therefore constructs its bus inside the zone its error handling lives in, for example inside `runZonedGuarded` before `runApp`.
- Operators an application chains onto an observed stream run their timers in the provider zone when RxDart creates them during delivery, and in the application's zone when it creates them at `listen`. nidana guarantees virtual time only for what the bus and topology pipelines schedule.
- An application's own RxDart `switchMap` that switches while its previous inner stream is live resubscribes only from the root microtask queue: on a TestBus after the settle operation, and never within a `testWidgets` body or a `fakeAsync` block.
- nidana.runtime owns the live `switchMap`, including what it does with an error from the previous inner's cancel; every other live combinator is RxDart's.
- The production provider adds no zone of its own, so a bus used from its construction zone delivers as a bare subject would.
- bus-api@v1 states the two providers, the zone rule for `bus.observe` and observers, timer cancellation at shutdown and the clock accessor as guarantees; bus-testing@v1 states the scheduler operations, quiescence, the bounds, the fixed start instant and the settle operation. Their behavior fixtures run on the VM and need no `fake_async`.
- Inside `testWidgets` the provider clock starts at the wall-clock time of the test start, so widget tests compare timestamps as differences.
- flutter-binding@v1's widget-test helpers own bus shutdown at unmount. navigation-api@v1's executor protocol stamps route transitions from the bus's clock.
- A per-topic dispatcher hint in a topic declaration can have no effect on Dart; topic-model@v1 carries none (its G5, which resolved OQ-M3).
- No v1 lint enforces item 8. A phase gate can search the sources for `DateTime.now(`, `Stopwatch(` and timers created outside the provider implementation.
- Marked for upstreaming: Spec §3.6 and Guide §6.5 do not say how scheduler injection works on Dart, and Guide §6.5 names an `EventLoopScheduler` that RxDart does not have; Guide §10.1's clock-control question has a Dart answer here; Spec §2.3 says the bus implements no schedulers and no stream combinators, while on Dart the test scheduler and the live `switchMap` are nidana's, because RxDart has no scheduler and its `switchMap` escapes every fake-time zone; Spec §11.2's "in `tearDown`" for tests does not hold under flutter_test, which needs the bus shut down before the test body ends.

## Alternatives considered

- **Reimplementing every time-shifting combinator against an abstract scheduler interface.** It contradicts Spec §2.3 ("The bus does not implement schedulers, backpressure, or stream combinators") and duplicates RxDart, while the zone already reaches every timer RxDart creates.
- **Relying on `fake_async` alone.** It fails whenever the bus is constructed or activated outside the fake zone, leaves Spec §3.6's `SchedulerProvider` without a Dart meaning, and misses the replay and deferral microtasks of subscriptions made elsewhere.
- **Wrapping `package:fake_async` as the virtual scheduler.** It is mature and already in every Flutter resolution, but it would add a dependency to a published package that ADR-002 leaves out; its default start time is the wall clock; its `elapse` never returns on a zero-period periodic timer; and it has the same root-zone hop. The signal that would favor it: nidana's own scheduler failing a timer case that `fake_async` already handles.
- **A pass-through fork as the production zone**, as the root proposed. It behaves the same, but costs about 1.3 times as much per delivery when the bus runs in the root zone and buys nothing the captured zone lacks. The signal that would favor it: a need to tell bus callbacks apart by zone, such as zone-local diagnostics.
- **Reading `clock` at each call instead of capturing it.** A publish made under a different `withClock` would stamp a clock other than the one the bus's timers follow.
- **`bus.observe` returning the subject's stream unchanged.** Subscribers would keep their own zone, but on a TestBus their replayed first value and the timers their chained operators create during delivery would escape virtual time. The signal that would favor it: a consumer that needs its own zone values inside its callbacks.
- **Asynchronous scheduler operations** that yield a real event-loop turn at every virtual instant. They would tolerate any root-zone hop, RxDart's `switchMap` and application-chained operators included, but every operation would need `await` and a real timer per instant, and they would fix the TestBus only, not `fakeAsync` blocks or `testWidgets` bodies. The signal that would favor them: a live combinator whose work cannot be kept inside the provider zone.
- **RxDart's `switchMap` unchanged, with the settle operation after every publish.** Its resubscription would still miss every virtual instant that `advanceTimeBy` reaches before the next real turn, and would never run inside a `testWidgets` body.
- **A TestBus inside widget tests.** It works, but its clock is independent of `tester.pump`, so every such test would drive two clocks.
- **An open `SchedulerProvider` interface** that applications implement. Constructing the bus inside a custom zone already gives an application its own timers and clock through the production provider, while a hand-written provider could break the quiescence and cancellation rules the bus relies on.
